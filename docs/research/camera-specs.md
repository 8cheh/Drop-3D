## 2. Dimensionless numbers actually reported

### 2a. Capillary number Ca = µU/σ for SLIDING and ROLLING drops

| Value / range | Exact wording or context | Paper | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ca < 10⁻⁵** (and **We < 10⁻⁵**) | "For a drop with radius R ≈ 1 mm, both the capillary number Ca = ηV/γ and the Weber number We = ρV²R/γ are smaller than 10⁻⁵, implying that drops keep a quasi-static shape with contact size *l* independent of *V*, despite the motion." Drop speeds studied up to ~2 cm/s. | Backholm et al., PNAS **121**, e2315214121 (2024) | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11047067/ |
| **Ca ≤ 10⁻⁴** | "We slide grounded and insulated drops on hydrophobic surfaces at low capillary numbers (≤ 10⁻⁴)." | Hinduja et al., arXiv:2602.03362 (2026) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03362 |
| Deformation onset at **Ca ~ 10⁻²** | "Water subjected to a viscous force will elongate, which is indeed what we find when substrates are inclined by ~20° or more. The critical capillary number at which such deformations are found is on the order of 10⁻²." | Mouterde et al., PNAS **116**, 8220 (2019) | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6486785/ |
| **Ca > 10⁻³** = "large enough" for hysteresis to vary | "significant variations of the hysteresis are only expected at large enough capillary number (Ca > 10⁻³), far above the range of our experiments." | Backholm et al., PNAS **121**, e2315214121 (2024) | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11047067/ |
| **Ca ∼ (Bo − Bo_c)/c(θ)** — the sliding-drop scaling law | "Ca ∝ (Bo − Bo_c)/c(θ_d)", with Ca = ηU/γ_LG. Numerical values of Bo_c are in the paper's figures. | Varagnolo et al., PRL **111**, 066101 (2013); Sbragaglia et al., PRE **89**, 012406 (2014) | https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1305.1162 ; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.4803 |
| Ca = Uη/γ used as the abscissa for friction collapse | Friction force per unit width vs Ca: "It is equivalent to friction force per unit width and surface tension versus capillary number, Ca = Uη/γ." Drop velocities up to **0.7 m/s**; η from 10⁻³ to 1 Pa·s. | Li et al., Nat. Commun. **14**, 4571 (2023) | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10387105/ |

**Synthesis (my reading, clearly labelled as such):** the published evidence supports **Ca ~ 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁴ for slow sliding/rolling drops** (mm-sized drops at mm/s–cm/s, Ca < 10⁻⁵ measured on ultraslippery surfaces; Ca ≤ 10⁻⁴ on hydrophobic surfaces), and **Ca ~ 10⁻³–10⁻² as the upper end** where shape deformation and dynamic-wetting transitions set in. The "Ca ~ 1e-6 to 1e-3" range in the request is consistent with the literature, with 10⁻² being the transition ceiling.

### 2b. Bond number Bo for SLIDING drops

Definitions verified verbatim:
- Ca-based sliding-drop studies use **Bo = (3V/4π)^(2/3) ρ g sin α / γ_LG**, and define the **critical Bond number Bo_c = (3V/4π)^(2/3) ρ g sin α_c / γ_LG**, where α_c is the critical pinning angle. Source: Sbragaglia et al., PRE **89**, 012406 (2014), Eq. (1) region — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.4803 ; and Varagnolo et al., PRL **111**, 066101 (2013) — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1305.1162
- **Numeric Bo / Bo_c values: UNVERIFIED.** The values live in figures (Fig. 4 of the PRL; Fig. 4 of the PRE) and in tables that did not survive HTML conversion, and Cambridge Core / APS blocked full text.

For IMPACTING drops and slow drops, Bo is defined the standard way:
- **Bo = ρgR²/σ**, reported as **0.01–0.09** for the impacting silicone-oil drops in Harris et al., arXiv:2510.02220 (with **Oh = 0.02–0.5**). https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02220v2
